# Supabase setup and release status

The app uses email/password accounts. One owner creates each business and can give managers access with one-time codes. Each account is connected to at most one business. The CLI-linked Supabase project and GitHub repository are production resources. Accounting development uses a separate Supabase project named `arus-accounting-dev` in Singapore. The first twenty-one migrations were applied to production on 26 November 2026 after a dry run and a backup of the prior public data. Six later migrations for the unified cash workspace, archive lock, manager cash sale, journal IDs, routine classification, and sales analytics were applied to production after passing development smoke checks. The demo business was then seeded and its trial balance and registers reconciled; the test business Kedai Farrel was removed after backup. Private backups and seed scripts remain in Git-ignored `.cutover/`.

All twenty-seven checked-in migrations are applied to `arus-accounting-dev`. A rollback-only smoke test passed for account-template seeding, opening-balance restrictions, balanced posting, duplicate and unbalanced rejection, trial-balance and ledger amounts, owner authorization, denial of direct journal writes, legacy cutover locks, manager cash-sale permission, and owner-data cleanup. An earlier authenticated API smoke test passed for contacts, credit documents, payments, product stock, moving-average HPP, stock counts, fixed assets, monthly depreciation, and journals, then deleted its temporary development users. The development project credentials are stored in Git-ignored `.env.accounting-dev.local` with file mode 0600.

1. Run `npm run dev:accounting` from `Web`. The launcher verifies that the development project ref differs from the CLI-linked production ref and puts the development public settings in the Next.js process environment. It does not pass the database password to Next.js.
2. In the development project dashboard, configure email/password signup and the local redirect URL before testing browser signup. Open `http://localhost:3000/login`, create a development-only owner account, then create a business. Open **Akuntansi → Daftar akun** to check the 15 default accounts. If the business has existing balances, post **Saldo pembuka** before its first accounting transaction. The transaction forms cover cash revenue, cash expense, capital contribution, owner draw, and transfer. **Kontak** stores customers and suppliers. **Penjualan & pembelian tempo** creates linked documents and posts partial or full payments. **Produk & stok** records products or services, reconciles opening stock against the inventory ledger, and books cash or credit stock purchases/sales and stock counts. **Aset tetap** reconciles opening asset costs and accumulated depreciation, records new acquisitions, and lets the owner calculate and post one month of depreciation at a time. **Buku besar & neraca saldo** reads posted journals by date.
3. Keep any later `supabase db push` or `supabase db query` explicitly addressed to the development project ref. Do not use the checkout's default `--linked` target without an explicit `--project-ref`; it points to production. The accounting migration does not migrate historical cash transactions or create opening accounting balances.

Use a different password from any one pasted into a chat or log. To add another business owner, use **Buat akun** with a different email address and create a separate business. Email confirmation and password recovery by email require custom SMTP for addresses outside the Supabase project team; set that up before enabling those flows. [Supabase Auth configuration](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/general-configuration), [SMTP guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-smtp)

The migrations create `businesses`, `transactions`, `obligations`, `business_members`, and `business_invites` with row level security. Owners control settings and access; managers can record/correct transactions and obligations, record payments, and read the shared dashboard. The projection page is owner-only. Invitation codes are stored only as hashes. The third migration publishes business changes to Supabase Realtime so open pages update across accounts; the app also refreshes when the tab regains focus or visibility. The fourth migration adds a separate obligation date, backfills existing records from their creation date in Jakarta time, and prevents corrections below payments already recorded. The fifth migration blocks future entry dates and new unlinked payment categories, and allows scoped deletion of transactions and obligations without linked payments. There is no demo-data import: a new business starts empty.

The sixth migration adds accounting accounts, posted journal headers and lines, RLS, a default account template, and an owner-only posting RPC. The seventh migration restricts opening balances to one early balance-sheet journal and adds owner-only trial-balance and ledger queries. The eighth migration allows business deletion to clean up journal lines while retaining protection against direct deletion of used accounts and journals. The local accounting forms call the posting RPC, but the old cash ledger and projections are still separate until an explicit reconciliation and cutover.

The ninth migration adds owner-only contacts, immutable credit sale and purchase documents, linked payments, atomic journal posting, outstanding-balance reports, and protections against direct document-source journal creation. The tenth migration changes read-only report RPCs to invoker rights; their owner checks and row-level security remain active. Before the inventory or fixed-asset register is activated, the generic credit purchase form may record only value; those amounts must be allocated to detailed items before activation. After activation, use the dedicated stock or asset form.

The eleventh migration adds owner-only products and services, an inventory opening allocation, immutable stock movements, cash or credit stock purchases and sales, moving-average HPP, and stock count adjustments. Once activated, a deferred constraint checks that every posted inventory-account change matches product stock value. The accounting inventory book does not import or alter the old cash-flow tables. SAK EMKM financial statements remain later work.

The twelfth migration keeps a positive Rupiah stock value until the final unit is issued and allows a zero-value quantity difference at stock count to appear in the stock card without inventing a Rupiah journal.

The thirteenth migration adds an owner-only fixed-asset register, opening gross-cost and accumulated-depreciation reconciliation, cash or credit acquisitions, land without depreciation, and monthly straight-line calculation. A deferred constraint keeps gross asset and accumulated-depreciation account balances equal to the register. The owner explicitly triggers each month; a scheduler, asset disposal, and useful-life revisions are not included yet.

The fourteenth migration prevents new asset acquisitions from being backdated into completed depreciation months. Acquisitions in the current month remain possible and can be included by running that month again; already-posted asset depreciation is not duplicated.

The fifteenth migration adds owner-only monthly closing and audited correction for the five basic cash journal types. Months close in order, starting with the first journal month and only after the calendar month ends. Closed months reject journal and stock movement inserts. A correction in an open month retains the original journal, posts an exact reversal and a validated replacement in the same month, and records the reason, user, and timestamp. Credit documents, stock, and fixed assets require their own domain-aware correction flows; the basic correction screen does not accept them. `node scripts/accounting-dev-db.mjs dry-run` checks the migration target, `apply` pushes only pending migrations to the separate development project, `test` runs the rollback-only closing test, and `advisors` checks its security warnings. The script refuses the CLI-linked production project ref.

The sixteenth migration serializes account-name and active-status changes with period closing, so a simultaneous edit cannot change labels after the close snapshot is recorded.

The seventeenth migration adds audited correction for standalone credit documents and their linked payments in an open month. It keeps original journals, posts reversal and replacement journals, and prevents a document amount from falling below its payments. Documents linked to stock or fixed assets are excluded because their register values depend on the original document.

The eighteenth migration adds audited correction for the latest cash purchase, sale, or stock count of a product. It restores the stock quantity and value before the original movement, reverses its journal where present, and posts a replacement movement and journal. Credit-linked movements and movements followed by another movement of the same product cannot be corrected through this form.

The nineteenth migration adds audited correction for cash-acquired fixed assets before any depreciation has been posted. It replaces the acquisition journal and asset fields together. Credit-acquired and already-depreciated assets require a later correction flow.

The twentieth migration adds a stable posting order to stock movements, including reversal and replacement entries created in one transaction. The stock card and latest-movement correction guard use this order. Run `node scripts/accounting-dev-db.mjs test-credit`, `test-stock`, and `test-asset` for rollback-only domain correction tests on the development project.

The twenty-first migration lets the owner attach customer and supplier documents to the opening balance in one atomic operation. Outstanding details must equal each receivable/payable control account; the documents do not recognize revenue or expense again. They appear in the credit list and accept linked payments. Run `node scripts/accounting-dev-db.mjs test-opening` for a rollback-only reconciliation, payment, and owner-isolation test. The demo opening balance at 30 October 2026 was posted separately from the provisional cutover worksheet. Production backups and seed records are kept in Git-ignored `.cutover/`.

The local **Laba rugi & posisi keuangan** page compares year-to-date results with the same period in the preceding year and positions at the same calendar date in the preceding year. It reads four owner-authorized trial-balance snapshots; earlier year-end snapshots remove income and expenses from previous years. Both position columns and the account-level notes must reconcile before the page shows amounts. The current asset register appears in the notes only when its totals match the ledger; historical dates use account detail because the register stores current values. The owner-only PDF and Excel downloads use the same selected date and reconciled report data; the files include income, financial position, and notes with draft status and a Jakarta generation timestamp. The package remains a draft until opening balances, adjustments, business-specific notes, and the final accounting policy review are complete.

Migrations twenty-two through twenty-six switch the shared dashboard to posted journal cash and credit documents after the first accounting journal, lock the old cash and obligation tables against further editing, allow a manager to post only a cash sale with validated account roles, expose journal IDs for internal-transfer classification, and classify routine cash for projections including correction reversals. The former cash and obligation tables are kept for read-only archive access. `node scripts/accounting-dev-db.mjs test-foundation` runs the rollback-only checks for these additions. The owner-only actual cash-flow report reconciles its closing cash with the trial balance.

For deployment, set the same environment variables in Vercel with `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` set to the deployed origin. Review the email confirmation and account recovery policy before using real business data.

The twenty-seventh migration adds owner-only product and customer sales summaries. Product revenue comes from linked sale journals or credit documents, net of stock correction reversals; customer amounts come from credit sale documents, excluding opening balances. Cash sales without a product or customer link remain unattributed. `test-stock` and `test-credit` check corrected totals and access restrictions.
